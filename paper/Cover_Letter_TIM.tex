\opening{Dear Editor-in-Chief,}

We submit the regular paper ``Pure-Inertial Camera Motion Estimation from
Continuous IMU Streams'' for consideration in the \emph{IEEE Transactions on
Instrumentation and Measurement}.

The paper addresses an instrumentation question: which part of a camera
displacement is observable from a continuous low-cost IMU stream, and which
part requires an acquisition constraint. We provide a leakage-controlled
reference and calibration chain with a simulated known-displacement test, a
digital twin with measured sensor parameters, and a compact physics-structured
IMU-only estimator. The experiments include protocol-matched reimplementations
of public inertial architectures, session-wise cross-validation, uncertainty
coverage, and translation trajectory error. Images are used for calibration,
training supervision and evaluation only; deployment inference is based solely
on the IMU stream. We report the real-data comparison as it stands: all learned
models beat zero motion, while the proposed network matches the larger public
architectures with one seventh of their parameters rather than outperforming
them significantly.

We make the following limitation explicit. The historical real-test recording
was excluded from gradient fitting and the initial fixed-split architecture
selection, but it was subsequently reused for reported exploratory
cross-validation-ensemble, stillness-gate and blend diagnostics. We therefore
call it ``held out'', not a one-shot sealed confirmatory test. Before
submission, this paragraph must be updated with the results of the frozen
prospective protocol (four new test recordings) or retained verbatim if those
recordings are not yet available.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, contains no
human-subject or clinical data, and has been approved by all authors. Software,
configuration, split manifests, the frozen study protocol and aggregate result
artefacts are available at
\url{https://github.com/liranyang123456-commits/EGO_Mo}; an archived release
with a DOI will be referenced in the final version.

The current IEEE two-column manuscript has 15 pages. We acknowledge the
mandatory overlength-page policy and will identify an author with financial
authority in PeerTrack.
