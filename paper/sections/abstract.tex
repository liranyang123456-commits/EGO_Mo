\begin{abstract}
Estimating metric camera displacement from a single low-cost inertial
measurement unit (IMU) is ill-conditioned: acceleration does not reveal the
initial velocity, and attitude errors leak gravity into translation. We present
a measurement framework for a handheld camera with an endoscopic field of view
in which inference uses only the 200-Hz six-axis IMU stream, while chessboard
poses serve solely for calibration, training supervision and evaluation. The
framework comprises 1) a chessboard reference characterized by repeatability,
corner-noise propagation, dynamic holdout, tilt--translation sensitivity and a
simulated known-displacement test; 2) a digital twin whose sensor and motion
parameters are fitted to the real recordings; and 3) PhysNet, a
0.42-M-parameter network that maps anchor-frame pre-integrated IMU features to
a densely supervised velocity stream, gates that stream by a learned stillness
probability, estimates the camera--IMU lever arm and predicts its own
uncertainty. Each 3-s displacement is decomposed into an IMU-observable double
integral and an initial-velocity term that the window does not determine.
Consistently, more than forty estimator variants share one error plateau
within the seed spread, and all predictions are shrunk toward zero (slope 0.2--0.5
against the reference); a velocity proxy from the context, however, does not
reduce the residual across recordings, so the share caused by the initial
velocity is not measurable with the 10-fps reference. Under
one protocol, PhysNet lowers the synthetic-test error of an adapted IMUNet by
24\% with one seventh of its parameters. On real data, leave-one-session-out
errors of the fold ensembles of gated PhysNet, IMUNet and their blends lie
between 24.5 and 25.8~mm against 30.4~mm for zero motion; on a held-out
recording all of them beat zero motion (49.7~mm) by 6--7.5~mm, but none differs
significantly from IMUNet. With integrated-gyroscope attitude and periodic
position anchoring, the translation stream gives a relative trajectory error of
80.6~mm against 119.2~mm for zero motion. A
zero-velocity boundary experiment shows that pauses help only over short
intervals under the present attitude solution, which motivates a pause-rich
protocol for prospective validation.
\end{abstract}
