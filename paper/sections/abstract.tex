\begin{abstract}
Estimating metric camera motion from a single low-cost inertial measurement
unit (IMU) is ill-conditioned because acceleration does not reveal the
initial velocity and attitude errors leak gravity into translation. We
propose an audited pure-inertial measurement framework for a handheld camera
with an endoscopic field of view. The framework combines 1) a chessboard
reference characterized by repeatability, Monte Carlo corner propagation,
dynamic holdout and tilt--translation sensitivity; 2) a
parameter-provenance digital twin that reproduces the measured sampling
jitter, three-axis noise, board-to-gravity orientation and real-motion
distribution; and 3) PhysNet, a physics-structured network that transforms
200-Hz IMU streams into a densely supervised displacement stream, applies a
detected zero-velocity phase as a structural update, learns the camera--IMU
lever arm and predicts calibrated uncertainty. We further decompose each 3-s
displacement into an IMU-observable double integral and an unobservable
initial-velocity term, which connects an empirical accuracy plateau to a protocol-dependent observability floor. More than forty estimator variants show no consistent improvement beyond the seed-to-seed variation, and reference measurements reduce the residual by
16\% (31.4 to 26.3~mm) when a low-frequency velocity proxy is regressed out.
A correctly identified pause supplies a zero-velocity boundary condition, but
the measured benefit deteriorates rapidly with interval length under the
present attitude solution; the current recordings contain only 0.5--2.3 such
pauses per minute. This yields a quantitative pause requirement for future
acquisition. Under a protocol-matched comparison,
PhysNet reduces synthetic-test error by 24\% relative to adapted IMUNet with
one seventh of its parameters and gives the lowest leave-one-session-out
error (25.6 versus 26.4~mm). On one held-out recording the methods are
statistically tied; their blend reduces the zero-motion error from 49.7 to
42.2~mm ($R^2=0.279$). The proposed framework therefore identifies what the
sensor can measure, provides an efficient estimator that reaches that limit,
and specifies which acquisition change can move it.
\end{abstract}
