\section{Parameter-Provenance Measurement Digital Twin}
\label{sec:twin}

Following the measurement-oriented view of digital
twins~\cite{wright2024dt}, every twin parameter is either
measured, specified, or explicitly marked provisional. The twin generates
stereo images, exact pose, an ideal IMU stream, and a noisy/quantized stream
on the same timestamps.

\subsection{Rigid-Body and IMU Model}
Let $W$ denote the inertial world frame used by the twin (the fixed board
frame plays this role in the real experiment). For IMU position
${\bf p}_{WI}$ expressed in $W$ and orientation ${\bf R}_{WI}$ mapping IMU
coordinates into $W$, ideal
specific force is
\begin{equation}
 {\bf f}_I(t)={\bf R}_{WI}^{\top}(t)
 [\ddot{\bf p}_{WI}(t)-{\bf g}_W], \label{eq:specific-force}
\end{equation}
where ${\bf g}_W$ is the gravitational-acceleration vector expressed in
$W$, including its sign. If ${\bf r}_{CI}^{C}$ is the vector from the camera
centre to the IMU sensing centre expressed in $C$, then
${\bf p}_{WI}={\bf p}_{WC}+{\bf R}_{WC}{\bf r}_{CI}^{C}$; its second
derivative contributes tangential and centripetal acceleration. The
exported accelerometer channel is ${\bf f}_I/g_0$ in units of $g$, with
$g_0=9.80665$~m/s$^2$. Angular rate is obtained from
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol\omega_I(t_k)=\frac{1}{\Delta t_k}
 \operatorname{Log}^{\vee}[{\bf R}_{WI}^{\top}(t_{k-1})
 {\bf R}_{WI}(t_k)] , \label{eq:gyro}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Log}^{\vee}:SO(3)\rightarrow\mathbb R^3$ is the
rotation-vector logarithm and $\Delta t_k=t_k-t_{k-1}$; the result represents
the average body rate over $[t_{k-1},t_k]$, resolved in the preceding IMU
frame.
The noisy sensor output is
\begin{align}
 \tilde{\bf f}_k &= Q_a({\bf f}_k+{\bf b}_{a,k}+{\bf n}_{a,k}),\\
 \tilde{\boldsymbol\omega}_k &=
 Q_g(\boldsymbol\omega_k+{\bf b}_{g,k}+{\bf n}_{g,k}),
\end{align}
where $Q_a(\cdot)$ and $Q_g(\cdot)$ are the component-wise quantization and
full-scale-clipping operators. The white noises
${\bf n}_{a,k}$ and ${\bf n}_{g,k}$ are zero-mean Gaussian variables with
their measured discrete-sample full three-axis covariance (no additional
$\Delta t_k$ factor); each bias follows
${\bf b}_{k+1}={\bf b}_k+{\bf w}_k\sqrt{\Delta t_k}$ with a zero-mean
Gaussian random-walk increment ${\bf w}_k$. Sampling intervals are
drawn from the measured jitter distribution and clipped to physically valid
bounds.

\paragraph{Parameter provenance.}
Camera matrices and distortion come from the physical calibration. The
camera--IMU rotation comes from the independent rigidity recording. White noise,
quantization and timing jitter are fitted to the first 5~s of a real static
recording; the bias random walk cannot be identified from so short a record
and is provisional. Board geometry is generated directly from its metric
specification, not reconstructed from images. The orientation of gravity in
the board frame is measured from the reference poses and the accelerometer:
the real board stands tilted, with the specific force along
$(0,-0.87,-0.49)$ in board axes, whereas the initial twin assumed a flat
board with gravity along the board normal. A ``replay'' motion family drives
the twin camera along smoothed real training trajectories (positions from
the reference, rotation from the integrated real gyroscope) at random speed
and amplitude, so that synthetic sequences inherit the real motion
statistics without using validation or test motion. The stereo baseline and
IMU lever arm remain provisional; this status is serialized with every
sequence.

\subsection{Scene, Motion Domain and Audit}
An OpenCV renderer provides fast pinhole-plus-distortion images and Blender
Eevee provides 720p--2K materials and lighting. Twelve motion families
(translation, rotation, stop--go, spiral, replay and mixed motion, among
others) are generated with lighting, blur, noise, speed, bias and covariance
scale randomized per sequence; board motion is an OOD condition because a
camera-mounted IMU cannot observe it. The selected corpus contains 60
training, 15 validation, 15 test and five OOD inertial sequences plus 25
rendered representatives; its audit is reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.
Two handheld families (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) follow a non-periodic random
spline at random speed, with or without 1--2-s stops, matched to the real
translation and angular rate (median 5.6--6.3 versus 4.3~$^\circ$/s).
