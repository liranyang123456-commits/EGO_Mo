\section{Conclusion}

We presented a measurement framework that estimates camera displacement from a
continuous 200-Hz IMU stream alone at inference and characterizes the chain
used to train and test it. Separating the 3-s target into an acceleration
integral and an unobservable initial-velocity term is consistent with the error
plateau shared by more than forty estimator variants and with their shrinkage
toward zero; longer context and session fusion did not recover the missing
velocity. A confirmed pause supplies it only over short intervals, and the
current recordings contain 0.5--2.3 pauses per minute. In a non-periodic
handheld twin, one firm stop every 4~s lowers the error by 32\% relative to
pause-free motion, against 9\% for the frozen 10--15-s spacing, while an ideal
IMU changes it little.

PhysNet combines analytic anchor-frame pre-integration, dense displacement
supervision, a stillness gate, lever-arm estimation and rescaled uncertainty.
It reduced the synthetic-test error of an adapted IMUNet by 24\% with one
seventh of the parameters. On real data, gated PhysNet, IMUNet and their blends
beat zero motion in cross-validation and on the held-out recording, but do not
differ significantly from one another; the equal-weight blend reached 42.2~mm
against 49.7~mm for zero motion. With integrated-gyroscope attitude and
periodic position anchors, the relative trajectory error is 80.6~mm against
119.2~mm. Additional real sessions with a continuously visible board are
difficult to acquire, so the acquisition comparison is made in a twin matched
to the measured motion and sensor domain. A physical displacement standard is
still required before any reference-grade claim.
