\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Reference Measurements for Camera Motion}
Learned pose estimators are scored against references that are produced
differently in each domain. Robotic
datasets read forward kinematics, as in StereoMIS, where a learned estimator
reaches 2.04~mm ATE on short surgical
scenarios~\cite{hayoz2023pose}. Laboratory benches add external optical
tracking: Hartwig \emph{et al.} combine stereo laparoscopy, handle-mounted
IMUs and infrared tracking, and use the gyroscope, gravity and the
remote-centre-of-motion constraint to leave only the insertion depth
unknown~\cite{hartwig2022constrained}. Public
endoscopic datasets often mix real and synthetic sequences, so metric depth
or pose is available only for the subsets whose generation or registration
process supplies it~\cite{ozyoruk2021endoslam}; visual-inertial benchmarks instead ship
the reference together with a continuous-time
calibration~\cite{burri2016euroc,furgale2013unified}.

None of these is available for a compact handheld camera used outside a
robot, which leaves a planar target. Its weaknesses are documented: the
first-order uncertainty of measurements from a
plane is classical~\cite{criminisi1999plane}, planar pose admits two nearby
minima and is ill-conditioned in
tilt~\cite{schweighofer2006robust}. Intrinsics follow Zhang's
method~\cite{zhang2000flexible}. We therefore treat the planar reference as an
instrument with a budget expressed following the GUM~\cite{gum2008}, and add
the measurement this literature implies but seldom reports: the translation
error produced by a one-degree tilt error at constant reprojection residual.

\subsection{Inertial Navigation and Learned Estimation}
Strapdown mechanization and its error propagation are classical: specific
force is rotated by the estimated attitude and integrated twice, so an
attitude error leaks gravity into the integrand and a bias enters
quadratically in time~\cite{titterton2004strapdown,kok2017using}. MEMS errors
are characterized by Allan variance~\cite{elsheimy2008allan} and
pre-integration makes the mechanics usable inside an
optimizer~\cite{forster2017preintegration}. The structural
consequence that matters here is standard in navigation yet usually implicit
in learned inertial odometry: over a finite window the accelerometer
determines a double integral, while the velocity at the window start is an
initial condition that no sample inside the window observes.

Pedestrian navigation removes that initial condition by exploiting the gait,
since the foot is periodically stationary and a zero-velocity update resets
the velocity and bounds the drift~\cite{foxlin2005pedestrian}; the detector is
a studied component~\cite{skog2010zerovelocity}, and error-state filters
refine how the update is applied~\cite{eskfzupt2024}. The window regressors reviewed below do not use a
stationary boundary condition, which may be unavailable during continuous
handheld motion. We instead detect the stationary epoch
and then \emph{measure} what it is worth: the accuracy of
zero-velocity-aided integration on this hardware as a function of interval
length, and how often the instrument actually stops.

\paragraph{Learned estimators and calibration.}
IONet~\cite{chen2018ionet} replaced open-loop integration by a learned
regression from inertial windows, and
RIDI~\cite{yan2018ridi}, RoNIN~\cite{yan2020ronin} and TLIO~\cite{liu2020tlio}
developed this into pedestrian benchmarks, where a strong periodic gait prior
makes displacement predictable; a recent survey reviews the
field~\cite{chen2024survey}. RoNIN and TLIO rotate the IMU samples into a
gravity-aligned frame using an external or filtered attitude, and AirIO shows
that the choice of input frame changes what the network can
observe~\cite{qiu2025airio}. A parallel line learns sensor corrections instead
of motion: gyroscope denoising for open-loop
attitude~\cite{brossard2020denoising} and AI-IMU dead reckoning, where a
network adapts the noise covariance of kinematic pseudo-measurements~\cite{brossard2020aiimu}.
Two TIM contributions are closest to our setting: IMUNet~\cite{zeinali2024imunet}
targets efficient inertial odometry on edge devices, and
DUET~\cite{liu2023duet} learns scale-factor, misalignment and bias corrections
from externally measured pose increments.

These works rank architectures by accuracy but do not separate the error that
the sensor determines from the error caused by the unobserved initial
velocity, which is shared by every architecture. When the second term
dominates, differences between architectures can be smaller than the spread
between training seeds. We therefore report the error floor and the
acquisition factors that could lower it alongside the architecture
comparison, together with parameters, latency and transfer from simulation,
in line with the metrological characterization of location-estimation
algorithms in the I\&M literature~\cite{carbone2015navigation}.

\subsection{Uncertainty and Digital Twins}
A measurement result is complete only with its uncertainty~\cite{gum2008}. The two
standard ingredients are a heteroscedastic likelihood for the input-dependent
aleatoric term~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties} and an ensemble for the
epistemic term~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}; both require empirical
coverage assessment, and a single variance scale fitted on validation can
recalibrate them~\cite{laves2020well}. Because overlapping windows are
strongly dependent, confidence intervals use a block bootstrap over
non-overlapping blocks~\cite{carlstein1986use,kunsch1989jackknife}.

\paragraph{Digital twins and simulation.}
Metrology-oriented twins require explicit links between models, measured
parameters, uncertainty and decisions~\cite{wright2024dt}. Zimmer
et~al.~\cite{zimmer2025digital} use a data-driven twin to produce
pseudo-measurements where a physical sensor is absent; hybrid
simulation--experiment datasets require faithful physical and noise
models~\cite{fu2023hybrid}, and deep measurement systems still need controlled
studies of the physical excitation~\cite{evans2025pattern}. Our twin measures
its parameters, including the attitude of gravity in the board frame, replays
real trajectories, and is never substituted for real-test accuracy.
