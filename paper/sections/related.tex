\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Reference Measurements for Camera Motion}
A learned pose estimator is only as meaningful as the reference it is scored
against, and that reference is produced differently in each domain. Robotic
datasets read forward kinematics, as in StereoMIS, where a learned estimator
reaches 2.04~mm ATE on short surgical
scenarios~\cite{hayoz2023pose}. Laboratory benches add external optical
tracking: Hartwig \emph{et al.} combine stereo laparoscopy, handle-mounted
IMUs and infrared tracking, and observe that inertial integration alone does
not recover the insertion direction~\cite{hartwig2022constrained}. Public
endoscopic datasets often mix real and synthetic sequences, so metric depth
or pose is available only for the subsets whose generation or registration
process supplies it~\cite{ozyoruk2021endoslam}; visual-inertial benchmarks instead ship
the reference together with a continuous-time
calibration~\cite{burri2016euroc,furgale2013unified}.

None of these is available for a compact handheld camera used outside a
robot, which leaves a planar target. Its weaknesses are documented but rarely
audited in learning papers: the first-order uncertainty of measurements from a
plane is classical~\cite{criminisi1999plane}, planar pose admits two nearby
minima and is ill-conditioned in
tilt~\cite{schweighofer2006robust}, an ambiguity that also affects the fusion
of chessboard poses with inertial sensors. Intrinsics follow Zhang's
method~\cite{zhang2000flexible}. We therefore treat the planar reference as an
instrument with a budget expressed following the GUM~\cite{gum2008}, and add
the measurement this literature implies but seldom reports: the translation
error produced by a one-degree tilt error at constant reprojection residual.

\subsection{Inertial Navigation and Learned Estimation}
Strapdown mechanization and its error propagation are classical: specific
force is rotated by the estimated attitude and integrated twice, so an
attitude error leaks gravity into the integrand and a bias enters
quadratically in time~\cite{titterton2004strapdown,kok2017using}. MEMS errors
are characterized by Allan variance~\cite{elsheimy2008allan} and
pre-integration makes the mechanics usable inside an
optimizer~\cite{forster2017preintegration}. The structural
consequence that matters here is standard in navigation yet usually implicit
in learned inertial odometry: over a finite window the accelerometer
determines a double integral, while the velocity at the window start is an
initial condition that no sample inside the window observes.

Pedestrian navigation removes that initial condition by exploiting the gait,
since the foot is periodically stationary and a zero-velocity update resets
the velocity and bounds the drift~\cite{foxlin2005pedestrian}; the detector is
a studied component~\cite{skog2010zerovelocity}, open implementations
and modern filters refine how the update is
applied~\cite{eskfzupt2024}. The window regressors reviewed below do not use a
stationary boundary condition, which may be unavailable during continuous
handheld motion. We instead detect the stationary epoch
and then \emph{measure} what it is worth: the accuracy of
zero-velocity-aided integration on this hardware as a function of interval
length, and how often the instrument actually stops.

\paragraph{Learned estimators and calibration.}
IONet~\cite{chen2018ionet} replaced open-loop integration by a learned
regression from inertial windows, and
RIDI~\cite{yan2018ridi}, RoNIN~\cite{yan2020ronin} and TLIO~\cite{liu2020tlio}
developed this into pedestrian benchmarks, where a strong periodic gait prior makes displacement
predictable. A parallel line learns sensor corrections instead of motion:
gyroscope denoising for open-loop attitude~\cite{brossard2020denoising} and
AI-IMU dead reckoning, where a learned covariance activates kinematic
constraints~\cite{brossard2020aiimu}. Two TIM contributions are closest to our
setting: IMUNet~\cite{zeinali2024imunet} reports model size and edge latency
beside trajectory error, and DUET~\cite{liu2023duet} learns scale,
misalignment, bias and noise corrections from externally measured pose
increments while studying bias-domain shift and window length.

The common denominator is an accuracy ranking between architectures. What is
missing, and what the handheld endoscopic case makes unavoidable, is a
decomposition of the residual into the part the sensor determines and the part
that is an unobservable initial condition shared by every architecture. Under
our protocol more than forty variants---including protocol-matched reimplementations of the architectures above---show no consistent gain beyond the seed variation,
so such a ranking measures the noise of a saturated system. We therefore
report where the limit is, what moves it, and for the representation itself
the cost of reaching it: parameters, latency, data requirement, and
transferability from simulation.

\subsection{Uncertainty and Digital Twins}
Reporting a value without an uncertainty is not a measurement. The two
standard ingredients are a heteroscedastic likelihood for the input-dependent
aleatoric term~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties} and an ensemble for the
epistemic term~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, both known to be
imperfect approximations that require empirical coverage assessment. We adopt
this combination, fit one positive scale on validation only, and report coverage and
discrimination. Because overlapping windows are strongly dependent,
confidence intervals use a block bootstrap~\cite{kunsch1989jackknife}.

\paragraph{Digital twins and simulation.}
Metrology-oriented twins require explicit links between models, measured
parameters, uncertainty and decisions~\cite{wright2024dt}. Zimmer
et~al.~\cite{zimmer2025digital} use a twin to produce pseudo-measurements
where a physical sensor is absent, the role it plays here; model-driven
spectroscopy shows that simulation and experiment can form a hybrid dataset
once the physical model and the real noise are represented
faithfully~\cite{fu2023hybrid}; and Evans et~al.~\cite{evans2025pattern}
demonstrate that a deep measurement system still needs controlled studies of
the physical excitation. Our twin follows this line: every parameter is
measured, specified or marked provisional, the attitude of gravity in the
reference frame is measured rather than assumed, real trajectories are
replayed so that the motion statistics are inherited from the instrument, and
synthetic accuracy is never substituted for real-test accuracy. The audit also
reports the cost of getting this wrong: a twin whose angular-rate distribution
was three times the real one produced no usable transfer at all.
