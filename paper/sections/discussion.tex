\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Findings and Measurement Implications}
\emph{Observability and acquisition (RQ1, RQ3).} The decomposition in
(\ref{eq:decomp}) is consistent with the shared plateau and with the shrinkage
of every estimator: the initial velocity is not determined inside the window,
and neither longer context nor session-level fusion supplied it. Measuring its
share needs a higher-rate or independent velocity reference, because the
differentiated 10-fps reference is dominated by PnP noise. A confirmed pause
supplies it only over short spans; beyond about 2~s time-varying attitude
error and nonconstant bias dominate. In the twin, one firm stop every 4~s lowers the error by 32\% relative
to pause-free motion, against 9\% for the frozen 10--15-s spacing, whereas an
ideal IMU, a longer look-back or a 30-fps label rate change it by at most
2~mm; the present recordings contain too few stops to show this. Attitude
accuracy is the second factor, because it limits how long a zero-velocity
interval remains useful.

\emph{Reference (RQ2).} The board is highly repeatable when static, but its
dynamic quality depends on frame rate and axis. A small reprojection residual
does not exclude a tilt-induced translation error, and the simulated
known-displacement test shows a 1.4--2.5\% lateral scale error that depends on
the modelled blur. The reference is suitable for comparing estimators whose
errors are tens of millimetres, but it is not externally traceable.

\emph{Estimator and twin (RQ4).} Anchor-frame pre-integration and an
integrated velocity stream give the lowest synthetic and OOD errors and match
the larger public architectures on real data with one seventh of the
parameters. The synthetic margin comes mainly from periodic motion families,
in which the context reveals the velocity; on random-spline motion the
synthetic error reduction falls to the level seen on real data. The held-out
difference from IMUNet is below the roughly 3-mm resolution of 38 independent
blocks. Correcting the twin's board-to-gravity
attitude and replaying real motion enabled useful direct transfer but did not
improve the fine-tuned model, nor did a non-periodic handheld twin with
motion-class-balanced pretraining. The remaining domain gap may arise from
unmodelled latency, vibration, scale and misalignment, and temperature.

\paragraph{Implications.}
Mount rigidity must be tested over increments long enough to exceed PnP noise,
and the input sensor must not be used to correct its own labels. Local and
trajectory metrics can disagree: the stillness gate improved every local
statistic while worsening trajectory error, so both are needed to select an
operating point.

\subsection{Limitations and Next Measurements}
Board flatness, rolling shutter and the true intrinsic error are not part of a
full GUM budget, and the reference has not been compared with an independent
tracker or a physical displacement standard. The real dataset has eight
trajectory recordings for training and validation, one held-out test recording
also used for post-selection diagnostics, and four rigidity recordings. The
frozen prospective protocol therefore requires at least four new sealed test
recordings on different days and with different operators or grips,
three-minute 10-fps sessions with 10--20~cm travel and a 1--2-s pause every
10--15~s, and a daily rotation/still recording that doubles as mount test; a
follow-up protocol should add a firm stop of at least 0.5~s every 4~s. A
complete inertial pose stream must also propagate its covariance.
