\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Findings and Measurement Implications}
\emph{Observability and acquisition (RQ1, RQ3).} The decomposition in
(\ref{eq:decomp}) is consistent with the shared plateau and with the shrinkage
of every estimator: the initial velocity is not determined by the samples
inside the window, and neither longer context nor session-level fusion
supplied it. How much of the residual it causes, however, cannot be measured
with a 10-fps reference, whose differentiated velocity is dominated by PnP
noise; a higher-rate or independent velocity reference is needed. A
confirmed pause does supply it, but only over short spans; beyond about 2~s the
boundary condition is outweighed by time-varying attitude error and
nonconstant bias, since a constant tilt is largely absorbed by the fitted
bias. More frequent pauses are therefore a hypothesis about how to lower the
floor, not a measured effect, and the frozen prospective protocol is designed
to test it. Attitude accuracy is the second factor, because it limits how long
a zero-velocity interval remains useful.

\emph{Reference (RQ2).} The board is highly repeatable when static, but its
dynamic quality depends on frame rate and axis, and a small reprojection
residual does not exclude a translation error caused by tilt. The simulated
known-displacement test shows a systematic scale error of 1.4--2.5\% on the
lateral axes, depending on the modelled optical blur. The reference is therefore suitable for comparing estimators
whose errors are tens of millimetres, but it is not an externally traceable
ground truth.

\emph{Estimator and twin (RQ4).} Anchor-frame pre-integration and an
integrated velocity stream give the lowest synthetic and OOD errors and match
the larger public architectures on real data with one seventh of the
parameters. The synthetic margin comes mainly from periodic motion families,
in which the context reveals the velocity; on random-spline motion the
synthetic error reduction falls to the level seen on real data. On the held-out recording the difference from IMUNet is not
significant; with about 38 independent 3-s blocks, the test cannot resolve
differences below roughly 3~mm. Correcting the twin's board-to-gravity
attitude and replaying real motion enabled useful direct transfer but did not
improve the fine-tuned model; the remaining domain gap may arise from
unmodelled latency, vibration, scale and misalignment, and temperature.

\paragraph{Implications.}
Mount rigidity must be tested over increments long enough to exceed PnP noise,
and the input sensor must not be used to correct its own labels. Local and
trajectory metrics can disagree: the stillness gate improved every local
statistic while worsening trajectory error through shrinkage, so both are
needed to select an operating point.

\subsection{Limitations and Next Measurements}
Board flatness, rolling shutter and the true intrinsic error are not part of a
full GUM budget, and the reference has not been compared with an independent
tracker or a physical displacement standard. The real dataset has eight
trajectory recordings for training and validation, one held-out test recording,
which was also used for exploratory diagnostics after model selection, and four
rigidity recordings. The frozen
prospective protocol therefore requires at least four new sealed test
recordings on different days and with different operators or grips,
three-minute 10-fps sessions with 10--20~cm travel and a 1--2-s pause every
10--15~s, and a daily rotation/still recording that doubles as mount test. The
translation-only trajectory comparison uses the reference orientation; the
pure-inertial relative pose instead uses integrated-gyroscope attitude, and a
complete inertial pose stream must also propagate its covariance.
