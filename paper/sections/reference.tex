\section{Uncertainty of the Chessboard Reference}
\label{sec:reference}

\subsection{Model and Type-A Repeatability}
For each left image the chessboard is detected and PnP returns
$({\bf R}_{CB},{\bf t}_{CB})$, mapping a board point into the camera frame.
The frame is kept if the reprojection error is at most 1.5~px and the board is
still; the camera pose used in Section~\ref{sec:framework} is
${\bf R}_{BC}={\bf R}_{CB}^{\top}$ and
${\bf p}_{BC}=-{\bf R}_{CB}^{\top}{\bf t}_{CB}$, and
physically implausible jumps (over 25$^\circ$ within 0.5~s or 40~mm within
0.2~s) are removed. Frames whose camera gyroscope stays below 1.5~$^\circ$/s
for $\pm0.25$~s are grouped into still intervals, and the pose standard
deviation inside an interval is the Type-A estimate $u_A$. To probe dynamic
consistency without assuming the IMU is correct, 20\% of accepted poses are
removed and predicted from the adjacent remaining poses by linear position and
geodesic rotation interpolation; this residual mixes measurement error with
unmodelled motion curvature and is therefore reported as an empirical upper
bound rather than a standard uncertainty.

\subsection{Corner Propagation and Tilt--Translation Coupling}
For 120 reference frames per recording, the board is re-projected from the
estimated pose, isotropic Gaussian noise is added to every corner, and PnP is
re-solved 60 times; the sample covariance is the Type-B contribution
$\boldsymbol\Sigma_{\rm corner}$, evaluated at
$\sigma\in\{0.1,0.25,0.5\}$~px, where 0.25~px is close to the
intrinsic-calibration RMS. Because reprojection is a residual and not a pose
uncertainty, each accepted rotation is additionally perturbed by 0.5$^\circ$
about one camera axis and translation is refit to the unchanged corners. The
ratios
\begin{equation}
 s_p=\frac{\|\delta{\bf p}\|}{\|\delta\boldsymbol\theta\|},\qquad
 s_r=\frac{\Delta e_{\rm px}}{\|\delta\boldsymbol\theta\|}
\end{equation}
measure the pose sensitivity and the ability of reprojection gating to reveal
it. The implementation evaluates $\|\delta\boldsymbol\theta\|$ in degrees, so
$s_p$ and $s_r$ are reported in mm/deg and px/deg, respectively.

\paragraph{Operational uncertainty.}
For a scalar pose component $q$ the reported standard uncertainty is
\begin{equation}
 u_{\rm op}(q)=\sqrt{u_A^2(q)+u_{\rm corner}^2(q)}, \label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
where $u_{\rm corner}(q)$ is the square root of the corresponding diagonal
entry of $\boldsymbol\Sigma_{\rm corner}$; the expanded value is
$U_{\rm op}=k_{\rm c}u_{\rm op}$ with coverage factor $k_{\rm c}=2$. It is called
\emph{operational} and not SI-traceable: board flatness, rolling shutter and
an independent physical displacement standard are not yet available. Dynamic
holdout residuals are reported separately and are not combined as independent
Gaussian terms.

\paragraph{Simulated known-displacement test.}
The digital twin renders the board, with the calibrated distortion, sensor
noise and illumination flicker, along a caliper protocol: 0--40~mm travel on
each camera axis, seven 5-s pauses, three traversals per axis. The images pass
through the same detector and PnP as the real reference, and each axis is
scored after fitting only its direction and origin, so scale errors remain in
the held-out residual. With exact intrinsics the held-out RMSE is 0.73~mm at
250~mm and 1.20~mm at 350~mm working distance, the axis scale error reaches
2.1\% and 2.5\%, and the cross-axis RMSE reaches 0.9 and 1.9~mm, while
repeatability is about 0.01~mm; the error is therefore systematic and
pose-dependent, not noise. Drawing the intrinsics from their calibration
standard deviations raises the median held-out RMSE to 1.0 and 1.4~mm, and the
95th-percentile lateral scale error to 10--19\%, so intrinsic uncertainty
dominates the budget; no working distance passes the 1\%/0.5-mm acceptance
limits. At the mean 3-s test displacement of about 50~mm, the 2.5\% nominal
scale error is about 1.2~mm, roughly 3\% of the 42--44-mm model errors in
Section~\ref{sec:results}; it does not change the method ranking but bounds
claims below the millimetre level.
